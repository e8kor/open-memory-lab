- **A distributed provider: one lead signs, a standby lead takes over, nodes serve the workareas placed on them** ([ADR-0073](adr/ADR-0073-a-leader-signs-every-write-and-nodes-serve-the-workareas-placed-on-them-each-read-routed-to-the-closest-less-busy-replica-fresh-enough-for-it.md), proposed). The lead keeps every tenant's signing chain and every write, and a standby lead with planned handover and a fold version window keeps the API up through restarts and rolling upgrades. A node runs a PostgreSQL of its own holding the units placed on it by weighted rendezvous hashing, fed by the signed journal up to the position the authority's synchronous standby holds, each event verified before it is applied and each join verified against a signed checkpoint. Read leases are bound to the lead's term and granted in step with narrowing commits, so a revoked grant, a denial, a demotion or an erasure applies on every node before its caller hears it took effect, through a lead failover too. A read goes to a healthy, leased replica fresh enough for the client's opaque position token, nearest tier first, and to the lead when none qualifies. The record states its clock assumptions, what a compromised node can and cannot do, when not to run more than one process, and its operational costs. Amends ADR-0023 d13, d14 and d16, ADR-0024 d9, ADR-0002 d2 and ADR-0042 d1 and d4; three phases, upgrades first, then locality, then placement roots and federated recall, tracked as issues [#209](https://github.com/e8kor/open-memory-lab/issues/209), [#210](https://github.com/e8kor/open-memory-lab/issues/210) and [#211](https://github.com/e8kor/open-memory-lab/issues/211); a Cluster page mockup, not built.
