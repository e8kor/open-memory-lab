Privacy notice

What this service keeps, and why

memory.doublegate.io · effective 4 November 2026

This notice covers the Open Memory Lab service at memory.doublegate.io. It describes what is stored when you or your agents use it, why, for how long, and what you can ask for.

Who is responsible

The controller is Eugene Korniichuk, Kraków, Poland. Write to privacy@doublegate.io for any question or request about your data.

No advertising, no analytics, and no third-party tracking scripts. The landing page loads nothing from other sites.

Legal basis

  • Providing the service you asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)): your account, sign-in and the memories you write.
  • Legitimate interest in running it securely (Art. 6(1)(f)): request logs and the signed history.

Who else processes it

  • Hetzner Online GmbH hosts the server, in Helsinki, Finland (EU).
  • Google or GitHub, when you choose to sign in with them. They tell this service your verified email address and account id; their own privacy terms apply to your account with them.
  • Cloudflare answers DNS for the domain. Your traffic does not pass through Cloudflare.

Data is not sold and is not shared with anyone else. Memories are never used to train models by this service.

What you can ask for

You can ask for a copy of your data, for corrections, for your access to end, and for your data to be erased. Write to privacy@doublegate.io; requests are answered within one month.

The service is built never to delete silently: removing a memory is normally a recorded step that takes it out of every read. Erasure that makes text permanently unreadable is not yet a built feature, so an erasure request is carried out by hand by the operator, who confirms to you in writing what was removed. The signed history keeps only digests and attribution, never the text.

You may also complain to the Polish supervisory authority, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO), uodo.gov.pl, or to the authority where you live.

Keep secrets out

Memories are meant for findings, decisions and rules. Do not store passwords, keys, or other people's personal data in them.

Changes

If this notice changes, the new version is published here with its date. The source of this page is public in the Open Memory Lab repository.
