Privacy notice

What this service keeps, and why

memory.doublegate.io · effective 4 November 2026

This notice covers the Open Memory Lab service at memory.doublegate.io. It describes what is stored when a person ("the user") or the user's agents use it, why, for how long, and what can be asked for.

Who is responsible

The controller is Eugene Korniichuk, Kraków, Poland. Any question or request about one's own data is sent to privacy@doublegate.io.

Legal basis

  • Providing the service the user asked for (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)): the user's account, sign-in and the memories the user writes.
  • Legitimate interest in running it securely (Art. 6(1)(f)): request logs and the signed history.

Who else processes it

  • Hetzner Online GmbH hosts the server, in Helsinki, Finland (EU).
  • Google or GitHub, when the user chooses to sign in with them. They tell this service the user's verified email address and account id; their own privacy terms apply to the user's account with them.
  • Cloudflare answers DNS for the domain. A visitor's traffic does not pass through Cloudflare.

Data is not sold and is not shared with anyone else. Memories are never used to train models by this service.

What can be asked for

A person can ask for a copy of their data, for corrections, for their access to end, and for their data to be erased. Requests are sent to privacy@doublegate.io; requests are answered within one month.

The service is built never to delete silently: removing a memory is normally a recorded step that takes it out of every read. Erasure that makes text permanently unreadable is not yet a built feature, so an erasure request is carried out by hand by the operator, who confirms in writing to the person who asked what was removed. The signed history keeps only digests and attribution, never the text.

A person may also complain to the Polish supervisory authority, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO), uodo.gov.pl, or to the authority where they live.

Secrets stay out

Memories are meant for findings, decisions and rules. The user must not store passwords, keys, or other people's personal data in them.

Each memory is scanned by a fixed set of rules when it is written. A private key, a known provider's API token, a user:password pair in a URL or after curl -u, or a password set in config (DB_PASSWORD=…) refuses the write, so it is never stored. Prompt-injection phrases, hidden Unicode and long base64 blobs are recorded as findings on the held memory. The rules match known shapes; a secret in another shape can pass, so keeping secrets out stays the user's part.

Changes
