# Roadmap

**Status:** design draft for review · decided, not built

This page is the honesty ledger. Nothing in Open Memory Lab is built, so there is no
"shipped" column to flatter: every row below is open, and the register exists so that every
limitation, open question and unmeasured claim in the documentation resolves to exactly one
row here with a verifiable exit criterion. The rules:

- A limitation is never deleted and never apologised for; it becomes a row, and it leaves the
  register when its exit criterion is met and verifiable — not when someone feels good about
  it. A row's number is never reused.
- An **RS** row is an open question the design has not answered. An **FR** row is a
  commitment: the design has decided what the answer must look like, and the row says what
  proves it.
- No measurement of OML exists. No page states a number for an unmeasured quantity; each such
  quantity resolves to a row here whose exit criterion will produce the number.

## Open questions

| ID | Statement | Exit criterion | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| RS-1 | Which embedding model the [search index](../architecture/retrieval.md) uses, and where it runs — inside the deployment or as an external endpoint — is undecided. The choice sets recall quality, cost, and what leaves the deployment's trust boundary. | A recorded decision naming the model, where it runs, what crosses the boundary to reach it, and how its output is erased when a memory is erased; FR-4's measurement then runs against that choice. | open |
| RS-2 | Agents authenticate to the [agent plugin](../api/mcp-plugin.md) with bearer API keys. The MCP specification defines an OAuth 2.1 authorization model; whether and when OML adopts it is open. Per the sourcing rules, this row is finalized only in the form: *spec revision X (fetched DATE) defines OAuth 2.1; client support as of DATE is [verified finding]; OML ships bearer keys; the OAuth trigger is [named condition]* — the bracketed parts are to be verified with fetch dates before publication. | The row restated in that form with real fetch dates, and the trigger condition recorded as a decision. | open — to be verified with fetch dates before publication |
| RS-3 | Federation — mapping claims across tenants into one "whole world" view, so that what separate organizations' crews learned can be related — is the stated end goal and is explicitly future. Nothing in the current design reaches across a tenant boundary; see [workareas](../architecture/workareas.md). | A design that states what a claim crossing a tenant boundary means for review, attribution and erasure, recorded as a decision; until then, every page that mentions the whole-world goal marks it future. | open — future |
| RS-4 | The Merkle construction over imported rows ([ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-dataset-onboarding.md)) — leaf ordering, hash construction, odd-node rule, canonical row serialization before digesting — is unspecified, and the root is a signed comparand: nothing about imports verifies until this is fixed. | A recorded specification with cross-implementation test vectors. | open — blocks FR-13 |
| RS-5 | Sampling-review semantics for imports: the server-assigned-seed ceremony, the published rate/confidence table, and whether sampling stratifies by a mapped column. | A recorded decision, and the escape-probability statement ((1−p)^n) placed where the review policy is documented. | open — blocks FR-13 |
| RS-6 | The import connector surface for the feature's first slice: pinned-file sources only (Parquet/CSV/Delta path by digest) versus live warehouse connections, and whether connectors are [ADR-0019](../adr/ADR-0019-hexagonal-architecture-and-plugins.md) plugins. | A recorded decision. | open |
| RS-7 | How manifest wrapping keys (MWK, [ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-dataset-onboarding.md)) interact with KEK rotation and the backup-decay bound — whether a retired MWK is destroyed on the same schedule. | An addition to [ADR-0005](../adr/ADR-0005-erasure-by-crypto-shredding.md)'s key-management table. | open |
| RS-8 | Whether erased-row digests are matched tenant-wide at every import, or only against the same dataset's prior manifests — the boundary of resurrection resistance ([ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-dataset-onboarding.md)). | A recorded decision either way, stated in the erasure inventory. | open |
| RS-9 | Tamper-evidence against an attacker holding the deployment signing key rests entirely on chain heads retained outside the deployment, and no mechanism yet exists for anyone to durably hold one — see [commitments](commitments.md). Whether OML publishes chain-head anchors (a transparency log, scheduled head export, or third-party anchor) is undecided. | A recorded decision naming the anchoring mechanism and its cadence, or recording the gap as accepted with the evidence-retention guidance stated where exports are documented. | open |

## Commitments

| ID | Statement | Exit criterion | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| FR-1 | Erasure destroys body and attachment bytes by destroying their keys; attribution metadata — who wrote it, when, into which workarea — survives erasure. The boundary is stated honestly on the [audit and lineage](../architecture/audit-and-lineage.md) page; closing it is this row. | A journalled erasure or pseudonymization act for attribution metadata, demonstrated end to end: after the act, the named fields — and, past the stated backup-retention + KEK-rotation bound, every wrapped-DEK copy — are unrecoverable from every copy class in the erasure inventory, and the evidence export reflects it. | open |
| FR-2 | Review throughput is unmeasured. The design's gate is only as useful as the review capacity behind it, and no number says what a deployment's reviewing crews can clear per unit time. A fast write path proves nothing about how fast review drains: if arrival outruns the drain rate, the held queue grows without bound and items dead-letter on expiry — which is why this row exists. | A measured drain rate — held memories cleared per second under a stated review policy, reviewer configuration and claim mix — published with date and setup, and compared against a stated arrival rate. | open — unmeasured |
| FR-3 | The lake mirror claims exactly-once application of every event across crashes and retries, resting on the Delta transaction-id mechanism as the Rust library (delta-rs) exposes it; see [storage](../architecture/storage.md). Whether the library behaves as the design assumes is unverified. | A conformance test that kills and restarts the lake writer mid-batch, under retries and failover, and verifies no event applied twice or lost — the claim is then written as "exactly-once, verified by conformance test T-n"; the library capabilities it rests on are cited from delta-rs documentation with a fetch date. | open — unverified |
| FR-4 | Search quality over the serving store is unmeasured: recall, latency, and the exact-versus-approximate trade for vector search have no numbers; see [retrieval](../architecture/retrieval.md). | Measured recall and latency at stated corpus sizes and tenant counts, published with date, hardware and index settings, and a recorded exact-vs-approximate decision justified by those numbers. | open — unmeasured |
| FR-5 | Lake tables hold encrypted bodies only. A decrypted export scoped to a named audience — the journalled act that would let an analytics consumer read promoted content — is designed as future and does not exist in the current design. | A journalled per-audience export: the act names its audience, decrypts only for it, appears in the evidence export, and leaves no decrypted copy outside the export it produced. | open — future feature |
| FR-6 | Rotating a crew's keys is key-by-key. A crew of many agents whose keys must rotate together — on compromise, on schedule — has no bulk act; see [roles and keys](../architecture/roles-and-keys.md). | A bulk re-issue act: one journalled act re-issues a named set of keys with grants carried over reproducibly, old keys revoked at the end of a stated grace window (the single-key rotation story of [roles and keys](../architecture/roles-and-keys.md), in bulk), demonstrated on a crew-sized key set. | open |
| FR-7 | The record store's scale ceiling is unmeasured — how many events, memories and tenants one deployment's PostgreSQL carries before it degrades — and the partition strategy past that ceiling is undecided. | A measured ceiling with date, setup and the load shape that produced it, and a recorded partitioning decision naming the threshold that triggers it. | open — unmeasured |
| FR-8 | Rate limiting is an approximation: each server replica enforces a tenant's limit divided by the replica count, so a tenant can briefly exceed its quota and the enforcement error grows with replica churn. The approximation is documented, not hidden; see [security](../architecture/security.md). | Either shared enforcement measured accurate within a stated bound, or a recorded decision keeping the approximation with its error bound stated where quotas are documented. | open |
| FR-9 | The scoped market claim on the [positioning page](positioning.md) — no provider ships structural, multi-party, evidence-bearing admission control — rests on a September 2026 market check, which also produced the finding that killed the claim's categorical form. It is stale the day it is read. | A live search re-run with a fetch date at publication time; the claim re-published, re-scoped or withdrawn against what the search finds, and this row re-opened at every subsequent publication. | open — blocks the positioning claim |
| FR-10 | The agent surface fails the only-memory test: recall excludes the caller's own held rows (and review latency is unbounded by design, FR-2), the MCP catalog has no get-by-id, supersession is HTTP-only, and an author cannot even propose retirement of its own stale claim. See [critical path](critical-path.md) M2. | `include_own_held` on recall (author-only visibility, R3 untouched), an `oml_get` MCP tool, `oml_supersede`, and a journalled author retire-proposal verb — specified in the API/MCP pages, implemented, and the agent-only-memory scenario passing end to end. | open — on the critical path |
| FR-11 | Retrieval counts rows (`k`), not tokens; agents assemble prompts under token budgets, and no parameter serves that. | A `max_tokens` recall parameter with per-hit token counts and deterministic truncation (steering rows first, then by rank), specified and implemented. | open — on the critical path |
| FR-12 | There is no batch write path and no client SDK: one memory per POST, and the resumable-upload flow spans MCP and raw HTTP with nothing wrapping it. | `POST /v1/memories:batch` with per-item idempotency records, plus generated Python and TypeScript clients from [openapi.yaml](../api/openapi.yaml) with hand-written wrappers for idempotency, uploads and pagination. | open — on the critical path |
| FR-13 | Dataset onboarding ([ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-dataset-onboarding.md)) is designed and explicitly post-v1: its smallest slice is pinned-file sources, full-replace mode, a hard row cap, no shared indexing. It must not start before FR-2, FR-3 and FR-7 have numbers. | The slice demonstrated end to end: manifest admission under sampling review, MWK keying, bulk lake materialization, Merkle-root reconciliation, and the resurrection fence passing a re-import-after-erasure conformance test. | open — post-v1, gated on FR-2/FR-3/FR-7 |
| FR-14 | Import performance and its retrieval cost are unmeasured: capture-side throughput (map + encrypt + quarantine), lake-side materialization rate, and the recall-quality impact of an indexed bulk import on a tenant's organic memories. | Measured capture and materialization rates with date and setup, and an FR-4-style recall measurement with and without a large indexed import in-tenant. | open — unmeasured |
| FR-15 | Admission-side open points have no rows of their own: scan latency is unmeasured, and ADR-0004's open choices — extractor per attachment type, upload-session limits, admitted media types, serve-binding expiry — are undecided. | Measured scan latency with date and setup, and a recorded decision for each named ADR-0004 open point. | open |
| FR-16 | Lake-writer throughput and Delta commit latency are unmeasured, and the single-writer ceiling's revisit trigger ("measured lag") has nothing committed to produce the measurement. The FR-3 conformance harness must also prove the Delta transaction-id recovery authority survives checkpointing, VACUUM and transaction-identifier retention — the Delta protocol permits `SetTransaction` expiry, which would silently reset recovery to "never applied". | Measured sustained events/s and commit latency with date and setup; the FR-3 harness extended with checkpoint-mid-sequence, VACUUM and restart cases, and the transaction-identifier retention setting pinned in [storage](../architecture/storage.md). | open — unmeasured; extends FR-3 |
| FR-17 | The erasure SLO's composing windows — vacuum cadence, backup retention, KEK rotation period — are named but never measured or verified as an end-to-end bound. | A demonstrated end-to-end erasure across every inventory copy class, timed against the composed bound, published with date and configuration. | open — unmeasured |
| FR-18 | Full-genesis reconciler runtime and quorum-synchronous commit latency were pointed at FR-7, whose exit criterion (events/memories/tenants ceiling) does not cover them. | Measured full-genesis fold time at stated journal sizes, and measured commit latency under quorum-synchronous replication, each with date and setup. | open — unmeasured; split from FR-7 |
| FR-19 | Vocabularies as governed claims ([ADR-0021](../adr/ADR-0021-ai-human-ontologies.md)): the v1 slice — prefix registry, term lifecycle with atomic deprecation-and-successor, the `oml:` relation set, releases with materialized is_a closure, the vocab scanner, the read surface and the `held_by` attestation — is designed, not built. | The slice demonstrated end to end: a vocabulary evolves through AI-proposed terms, a human-gated deprecation with `replaced_by`, and a human-gated release whose serve is a diff; a claim cites a term under `enforcement: annotate` and self-heals after a deprecation via `vocab.inactive_curie` details. | open — on the critical path after M2 |
| FR-20 | The retrieval value and cost of vocabulary expansion are unmeasured: recall quality with and without `expand_terms: descendants`, closure-table size at stated vocabulary shapes, mapping quality under the AI-only predicates, and the per-vocabulary human-verdict load under the shipped defaults (the ≈7/week design estimate, [ADR-0021](../adr/ADR-0021-ai-human-ontologies.md)). | FR-4-style measurements with date and setup for expansion on/off at stated vocabulary sizes, closure storage measured against the O(terms × depth) estimate, and the measured verdict load compared against the design estimate. | open — unmeasured |
| RS-10 | The content of `oml:`, the provider-published upper vocabulary (relation terms: `is_a`, `part_of`, `replaced_by`, `consider`, the mapping predicates) — how small it stays, and its versioning with product releases — is undecided beyond the names ADR-0021 commits to. | A recorded register of `oml:` terms with definitions, shipped as product data, with a versioning rule. | open |
| RS-11 | Importing an external ontology (an OBO release, a schema.org snapshot) as a dataset of terms rides [ADR-0020](../adr/ADR-0020-dataset-onboarding.md) with a `kind: term` mapping — post-v1, after FR-13, and the mapping-spec `kind` field is reserved now. What external identifier discipline maps onto CURIEs (prefix collisions with the source's own) is the open half. | A recorded import profile: source prefix handling, term_status mapping, hierarchy-edge import authority, demonstrated on a small public ontology. | open — post-v1, gated on FR-13 |
